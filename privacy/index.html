Whistlepost / Privacy

What Whistlepost knows about you

Last updated November 19, 2026

Short version: your location is used on your iPhone to find the track you are standing next to, and it is not sent to me. There is no account. The only thing recorded outside your phone is that a purchase happened.

Your location

If you allow it, the app reads your location while it is open and compares it, on the phone, with the railroad data stored inside the app. That is how it works out the railroad, subdivision, milepost and nearest crossing. Your location is not uploaded, logged or shared by the app. You can refuse the permission and tap the map instead; everything still works.

The background map is Apple Maps. Like any app that shows it, your iPhone asks Apple for the map of the area on screen. Apple's own privacy policy covers that.

Your saved spots

Spots and notes are saved in the app's folder on your iPhone. They leave it only when you export or share them yourself.

Purchases

Whistlepost Pro is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the payment; I never see your card or your Apple ID. To know that Pro has been bought, the app uses RevenueCat, a service that records App Store purchases. It receives the purchase record and an anonymous identifier made up for the app. It does not receive your name, your email, your location or your spots.

What is not in the app

No advertising, no tracking across apps or sites, no analytics of where you go, no account, no contacts, no photos.

Phone calls

The red button opens the Phone app with a railroad's emergency number. The call is yours: the app does not place it by itself and learns nothing about it.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects nothing from them, as it collects nothing from anyone.
